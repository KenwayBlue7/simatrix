# Changelog — Simatrix (repository root)

All notable changes at the Simatrix project root (spanning Module1, Module2, and the
topic deploy copies). Per-module changelogs live inside each module folder.

## 2026-08-01
- Added: `graphics_module_3_topic_2_2_conic_sections` now teaches the **focal sphere** — §6.2's own bridge from the cone to the curve, and the largest gap the syllabus audit of this date found. Step 4's reveal runs in two acts: first on the SOLID, where a sphere is inscribed in the cone until it touches the cutting plane (that touching point is the focus), the ring where it touches the cone is carried by a plane perpendicular to the axis (the tangent plane), and where that crosses the cut is the directrix; then on the sheet, where PF and PQ are measured with them. The learner meets neither piece of apparatus as an unexplained given any more. Recorded as ADR-089 with RULES.md §3.43–§3.44.
- Added: `focalSphereFor()` in that topic's pure data layer — the whole construction solved in the V.P. as two-dimensional trigonometry, returning null for the apex cut (no sphere exists) and a null directrix for the circle (the tangent plane and the cutting plane are parallel, which is exactly why a circle has no directrix and e = 0). `verify/conic-math.mjs` proves it the only way that means anything: it measures PF ÷ PQ at the vertices of the real section and demands §6.3's eccentricity, for every case from the circle to the rectangular hyperbola, and gets it to 1e-15.
- Changed: Step 4's reveal is one ten-stage sequence across both panes rather than five stages on the sheet, and the readout names the pane each stage happens in ("4 of 10 · The focus (on the cone)"), so a 3-D reveal narrated in a side panel still tells the learner where to look.
- Added: `verify/shipped-module.mjs` now hammers the cut's tilt with the focal apparatus fully revealed — a sphere, a wireframe, a fat ring, a plane and a fat line rebuilt 40 times — and holds the GPU buffer count flat.

## 2026-08-09 (i)
- Fixed: `graphics_module_2_topic_0_introduction_to_orthographic_projection` — **the gap between a view's heading and its drawing was computed from a fiction.** A caption's clearance added `|off|` — the dimension's offset — to the dimension's own coordinates, which is only headroom when the offset points the way the caption hangs. A VERTICAL dimension's offset is HORIZONTAL: it moves the mark sideways and adds nothing above the view. The Cylindrical Block's elevation carries a boss height thrown 37 mm clear to the LEFT, charged as headroom, which parked "Elevation" **39 mm** above a drawing it had to clear by two. The reach is now taken from where the mark really lands — `alignedDim()`'s own dimension-line ends for a linear size, the elbow for a leader — plus the value's lift and half its height, then 2.5 mm of clear air and half the caption (it is centre-anchored, so half of it hangs back towards the view). Clear air is now 1.9–5.2 mm where a view sets its own row. RULES.md §3.60 extended again.
- Added: same topic — the air between every caption and the nearest thing drawn under it is measured on the laid-out SVG and asserted from BOTH sides: never touching (≥ 1 mm) and never adrift (the tightest on each sheet ≤ 6 mm). Calibrated by restoring the offset-based clearance — all four objects fail, the Cylindrical Block at 39.4 mm.

## 2026-08-09 (h)
- Fixed: `graphics_module_2_topic_0_introduction_to_orthographic_projection` — **"Elevation" and the side-view heading sat at different heights.** The two views stand side by side on the same band of a first-angle sheet, so their names are one row of headings, but each caption was cleared against its own view's dimensions alone. The Cylindrical Block throws a boss height 37 mm clear of the elevation while its side view carries a single 12 mm lane, which put "Elevation" **45 mm above** "Right side view" over two drawings standing on the same line. The pair now takes the OUTER of the two reaches: whichever view needs the room sets the row and the other rises to meet it — no nudge on either, which would go stale the moment a dimension changed. The plan keeps its own clearance on its own side. RULES.md §3.60 extended again.
- Added: same topic — the row is asserted for all four objects (elevation and side caption within 0.6 mm of each other, plan well below). Calibrated by restoring per-view clearances: the Cylindrical Block fails at 49.0 vs 11.6 and the Stepped Block at 45.0 vs 11.6.

## 2026-08-09 (g)
- Fixed: `graphics_module_2_topic_0_introduction_to_orthographic_projection` — **a view's caption was centred on the view's whole bounding box, centre lines included, rather than on the drawing.** The Bearing Block's elevation showed it: its bore centre line reaches 7 mm past the lug on the left with nothing to balance it on the right, so "Elevation" printed 3.5 mm left of a view that is symmetrical about its own axis. Two axes, two boxes, which is the actual rule. RULES.md §3.60 extended.

Calibrated by restoring the old box — the Bearing Block fails at 3.50 mm and 1.00 mm.

## 2026-08-09 (f)
- Changed: `graphics_module_2_topic_0_introduction_to_orthographic_projection` — **a diameter is now the HYBRID mark: a slanted line through the centre, extended past the circle to an elbow, then a horizontal shelf with ⌀value level above it.** Arrowheads at both ends of the diameter, pointing outwards. This is the third and final form of the same decision today, and it keeps what each earlier one was right about: the line through the centre STATES the measurement (a diameter is a full width taken through the middle), while the shelf keeps what the learner reads off the feature. The bare through-centre line wrote its value turned, along its own slant, over a hatch of linework; the plain edge leader made a diameter look exactly like a radius apart from the letter, in a topic that is teaching that distinction.
- Unchanged, deliberately: a RADIUS still starts ON the arc and never reaches the centre, because a line drawn across the middle would say diameter.
- Added: same topic — assertions that the two heads of a diameter are 2r apart, both on the circle and collinear with the elbow (which IS "passes through the centre"), that a radius carries no second head, that the symbol and the kind of mark agree, and that the arrowheads on the paper are COUNTED against the authored set — a second head drawn by one guarded line would otherwise go missing silently. ADR-141 amended again; RULES.md §6.31 rewritten.

## 2026-08-09 (e)
- Changed: `graphics_module_2_topic_0_introduction_to_orthographic_projection` — **⌀ and R are now drawn the same way, as a leader with a horizontal shelf.** Arrow on the feature, slanting leg out to clear paper, short horizontal shelf, value level above it. The through-centre diameter added earlier the same day is withdrawn: it is a correct BIS/ISO construction, but it lays a dimension line and two arrowheads ACROSS the hole being measured, and this topic's subject is reading a drawing — the learner is being asked to look at that feature, so nothing they read may sit on it. One shape of mark for both symbols also means the learner compares the WORDS, which is the distinction being taught. The leg still runs along the feature's own radius, so a diameter's leader points at the centre when produced and a radius's arrow lands on its arc by construction. ADR-141 amended; RULES.md §6.31 rewritten.
- Removed: same topic — the `dia` primitive, `diameterDim()`, `DIM_STYLE.diaTextRun` and both renderers' diameter paths. Nothing replaced them: `emitNote()` and `dimensions3d.js`'s `leader()` already drew this shape for radii.
- Added: same topic — the mark's SHAPE is now asserted off the SVG rather than assumed. Every leader is three points with a horizontal last leg at least 4 mm long, exactly one value sits one lift above each shelf, and none of those values is turned. An even-odd ray cast against each view's outline polygons proves no elbow and no shelf lands on the object. Nine assertions across three objects, on top of the ⌀/R symbol audit.

## 2026-08-09 (d)
- Fixed: `graphics_module_2_topic_0_introduction_to_orthographic_projection` **used ⌀ on two features that are not complete circles**. BIS SP 46 / ISO 129-1 asks what the VIEW DRAWS, not what the feature is. The Cylindrical Block's boss is a 50 mm cylinder, so it was labelled ⌀50 — but the plate under it is only 40 deep, so the plan draws just the two arcs standing proud of the plate's edges, 147.5° between them. It is **R25**, and its leader was anchored at 225°, inside the plate, pointing at a stretch of circle that is not on the paper. The Bearing Block's slot was ⌀18; a slot end is a semicircular cap, so it is **R9**, which with the 16 centre-to-centre already on the plan is the pair that specifies the slot.
- Changed: same topic — every circular size now goes through `roundDim(centre, r, sweep, ang, out)`, which takes how much of the circle the view draws and returns a diameter at 360° or a radius leader below it. The symbol is computed, never typed. Four diameters and five radii across the four objects.
- Added: same topic — a `dia` primitive with its placement in `diameterDim()`, next to `alignedDim()` and shared by both renderers. A diameter is a line THROUGH THE CENTRE with an arrowhead at each end on the circle pointing outwards, the line running on past the second head, and the value written over that tail on clear paper — not at the midpoint, which is the middle of the hole it measures. A radius's arrowhead is computed as `centre + r·û`, so it lands on the arc by construction.
- Changed: same topic — the Method 1 oracle now measures each value's PERPENDICULAR distance from its dimension line, with the foot of the perpendicular required to land on the line. The old test measured distance from the line's midpoint, which is only the same thing for a linear dimension. Six new assertions check ⌀/R against the view's own primitives rather than against the authored label. Recorded as ADR-141 with RULES.md §6.31/§6.32.

## 2026-08-09 (c)
- Fixed: `graphics_module_2_topic_0_introduction_to_orthographic_projection` **jumped on the first frame of every view change**. Reported as an instant `camera.position.set` / `controls.target.set` that needed replacing with an eased lerp; it was not that either — the flight already lerped position and target together on an ease curve for 1200 ms, and every assertion about the settled view passed. Sampling the Front label's pane position on EVERY animation frame found a flight that was smooth for its whole length behind one enormous first frame: 71 px on the cylindrical block's climb to the plan, 219 px on the bearing block's drop to the side view, against neighbouring frames of a tenth of a pixel. Pressing a direction rebuilds the annotation layer before the flight starts, so the content box moves, and `focusOn()` re-aimed `controls.target` at the new centre in one assignment while the camera stood still — the flight's first `lookAt` then spent that whole swing in a single frame. The re-aim is now itself a 260 ms tween, which a starting flight cancels and takes over: the flight reads the target where it stands, which is still the old centre, and carries it the rest of the way as it always did. Worst frame is now 1.1–1.5× its neighbours, down from 27–245×. Recorded as ADR-140 with RULES.md §5.22 (amended) and §5.23.
- Changed: same topic — the guided-steps panel toggle moved 2 mm further in from the viewport's right edge. Right-anchored, so moving it left is a larger `right`; size, vertical band, styling and panel behaviour untouched.
- Added: same topic — a frame-by-frame continuity oracle for camera flights. Seven switches, sampled per animation frame, each frame compared with the LARGER OF ITS TWO NEIGHBOURS rather than an absolute distance: an eased flight's biggest frame is merely its fastest and its neighbours are nearly as big, whereas a teleport is one huge frame between two still ones. Calibrated by restoring the instant assignment — five of the seven fail, at up to 245×.

## 2026-08-09 (b)
- Fixed: `graphics_module_2_topic_0_introduction_to_orthographic_projection` **jumped when you scrolled in any of the four orthographic views**. Reported as a missing `controls.target` update on view change; it was not that — the flight lerps the target across its whole duration and copies it exactly on landing. The topic hung its "the learner has taken over" handler on `OrbitControls`' `start` event, and `OrbitControls` fires `start` for the WHEEL exactly as it does for a drag. One notch therefore handed the orthographic camera back to perspective mid-gesture, swapped the view's dimension set for the free-orbit one, and re-framed the scene — three changes under a pointer the learner had only rolled, with their zoom discarded in the hand-off. A drag is now detected as pointer movement past 3 px with exactly ONE pointer down: a click that never moves is not a turn, and a pinch is a zoom. Recorded as ADR-139 with RULES.md §5.21.
- Changed: same topic — `focusOn()` moves `controls.target` to the new content centre whenever the camera is idle, and flights and snaps finish through one `controls.update()` with damping turned OFF. Neither was the reported fault; both are the same class of defect and both are invisible until someone zooms. A stale target drags the part sideways under the scroll instead of growing it in place, and a damped update spends the inertia left by the last drag, so a flick followed by pressing Front creeps off square in the frames after the flight lands. RULES.md §5.22.
- Fixed: same topic — the **Front arrow pointed at a point in mid-air** on any part whose front face is not a full rectangle. It was aimed at the bounding-box centre, which on the Bearing Block's L is the empty paper above the base and beside the arm. `frontFaceAnchor()` now drops a column of rays down the part's mid-width, keeps the stretch that strikes material, and aims at the middle of the stretch nearest the box centre — returning that face's OWN depth rather than the box maximum, so a part with a boss is met at the boss. On the parts that were already right it returns the box centre again, unchanged. Its point stands off the face by 3% of the part rather than 5%, close enough to read as touching.
- Changed: same topic — the **Front label rides the middle of the arrow's shaft**, one arrow-head below it, and `rig.setFrontLabelFloor()` is deleted. The previous pass had settled the label-vs-dimension collision by measuring the dimension layer's box in the orchestrator and dropping the label below it; that removed the overlap and put the name out in clear paper with nothing connecting it to the mark — reported immediately as the arrow and label "floating away from the object". Halfway along the shaft the line runs into the chip and out the other side, the way a labelled leader reads, and there is no lane to contest. RULES.md §3.72 rewritten accordingly; ADR-132's amendment withdrawn in part.
- Changed: same topic — the Dimensions chip moves 3 mm back left, 2 mm net right of the benchmark inset.
- Added (verification): twelve assertions measure the zoom's FIXED POINT. Solving `after = s·(before − F) + F` from the dimension values' centroid before and after a scroll gives the point the view zoomed about; it must land within 8% of the pane centre, the direction must still be lit, and the scale must actually have changed — for each of Front / Top / Left / Right. Calibrated by restoring the old binding: eight of the twelve fail, the fixed point up to 1058% of a pane-width off centre. One more assertion proves a DRAG still drops the latched direction into free orbit, and the Front-mark probe now runs on four objects × three directions with a per-direction band, because the front face is only in the middle of the frame from the front. 196 assertions green.

## 2026-08-08 (b)
- Fixed: `graphics_module_2_topic_0_introduction_to_orthographic_projection` drew its dimensions in **two different systems at once**. The SVG sheet turned each value along its dimension line (Method 1, aligned); the dimension layer on the SOLID left every value level, on the reasoning — written into ADR-132 — that a CSS2D label is billboarded and "the aligned system's rotation is about paper". That reasoning was wrong. Aligned is not a property of paper, it is how a value is written against the line it measures, and one dimension set rendered two ways is two conventions mixed, which is the one thing Method 1 forbids.
- Changed: the placement now lives in ONE function, `alignedDim()` in the pure-data `objectData.js` beside `DIM_STYLE`. It takes a dimension and returns the ends of its dimension line, the angle its value is turned through, and where the value's centre goes; both renderers add their own origin and stroke it. Sharing CONSTANTS was never enough — each renderer carried its own copy of the same trigonometry, and one copy had a sign error that lifted every RE-READ value to the wrong side, printing it under its own line instead of above it. Recorded as RULES.md §3.71.
- Changed: the turn on the solid is set on an INNER span (`.vp-dim__text`), because `CSS2DRenderer` rewrites the outer element's transform every frame — the benchmark's own `.vp-value` / `.vp-value__text` pairing, adopted verbatim. The angle is a constant rather than a per-frame reprojection: the layer is only ever drawn for the direction the camera is AT, and at each of the four principal directions the view's own 2-D frame lands on the screen square.
- Changed: both renderers anchor a value on its CENTRE (SVG `dominant-baseline: central`, CSS2D's own 0.5/0.5), so `DIM_STYLE.textLift` is one derived number, `textGap + textHeight / 2` = 3.2 mm. A baseline-anchored value swings about a point below itself, so the same lift cleared the line on a horizontal dimension and cut through it on a vertical one.
- Fixed: four lane defects the analytic audit surfaced. The Shaft Support's overall length sat INSIDE its bolt pitch, so the pitch's extension lines had to cross the overall dimension line to reach their own lane — the exact crossing lane discipline exists to prevent; smallest is now innermost. Two treads of the Stepped Block, a lug and the gap beside it on the Bearing Block, and the Cylindrical Block's plate thickness and boss height are each consecutive stretches of one line, so they now CHAIN in a single lane head to head instead of stacking in two. The Stepped Block's middle tread had been sitting 2 mm above the block's top edge with its extension lines cutting through the corner they spring from; the boss height's lower extension line had been running back along 25 mm of the plate's top edge.
- Fixed: the Front label and the overall length both want the paper directly under the part — the label because the arrow foreshortens to a dot when you look down it — and they printed on top of each other on the default screen. `main.js` now measures the dimension layer's own box and calls `rig.setFrontLabelFloor()`. Composition belongs to the orchestrator; the alternative was importing one annotation leaf into another (RULES.md §3.72). **[Superseded 2026-08-09 — see below. The collision went away and the label went with it.]**
- Changed: a view's caption clearance is derived from what a dimension actually REACHES — its lane, plus the value standing one lift off it, plus half the value's height — rather than from a guessed 4 mm.
- Changed: the Dimensions chip moves 5 mm right of the benchmark inset, as asked. The vertical position, the gap and every chip style stay the benchmark's to the pixel.
- Fixed (verification): the no-overlap oracle measured through `getBBox()`, which reports a node's geometry in its OWN user space and **ignores the element's own transform** — so it was blind to precisely what Method 1 adds. A turned value came back axis-aligned and unturned, and two values crossing on the sheet measured as clear. Now `getBoundingClientRect()`, in screen space, after the rotation.
- Added: the oracle proves the convention rather than the wiring. For each object it finds, for every value on the sheet, a dimension line PARALLEL to it whose midpoint is exactly one 3.2 mm lift away (±0.15) — pairing by geometry, not by DOM order — asserts no value is turned past upside down, asserts that the object HAS vertical dimension lines and that their values turned with them, asserts leader notes stay level, asserts no value runs into a view's caption, and asserts the Front label clears every value on the solid. 163 assertions green.

## 2026-08-06 (j)
- Changed: the sheet's visible linework splits into **two weights**. Only the outer SILHOUETTE of each view now carries the heavy line (2.5 px); visible geometry INSIDE it - steps, shoulders, bore and slot edges, internal arcs - drops to 1.8 px. Supporting lines stay at 1.0. Three levels the eye sorts before it reads anything: where the object ends, what is on it, and what the sizes are.
- Note: this is a deliberate DEPARTURE from ISO 128 / BIS SP 46, which put visible outlines and visible edges in one type at one width - which is what `graphics_module_1_topic_1_1_dimensioning` draws and what the previous pass matched. It is the emphasis engineering textbooks print, and it is justified here because the topic's whole job is teaching a beginner to read a view: they have to find where the object ends before they can interpret what is inside it. Recorded so nobody "corrects" it back to the standard without knowing it was chosen.
- Changed: the split is carried by the DATA's defaults, not by per-primitive tagging - a bare `poly()` is a silhouette (every view's profile is one closed loop), a bare `line()` or `circle()` is internal geometry. One loop is closed and is not a profile, the bearing block's slot, and it says `'edge'` explicitly. Both weights are inked in the SAME press, because they are two weights of one idea and a separate stage would add a beat that says nothing new (RULES.md 3.53).
- Fixed: **`vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`, introduced in the previous pass, was silently breaking the drawing.** It makes Chrome compute `stroke-dasharray` in screen space, which disables `pathLength="1"` - so the draw-on's dash meant "1 px on, 1 px off" instead of "the whole path" and every outline rendered as a fine dotted line. The previous pass verified it by watching the dashoffset PROPERTY animate to 0, which it does; the property was fine and the picture was not. Line widths are now `calc(<benchmark px> * var(--ink-scale))`, where `--ink-scale` is 1 / (px per mm) published by `projectionSheet.js` on layout and on resize.
- Note on why a fixed millimetre width will not do: the sheet is `preserveAspectRatio`-fitted, so its scale depends on the drawing's own extent as well as the pane - measured at 1.95 to 2.30 px/mm across the four objects at one window size. Any single authored width lands somewhere in an 8% band. Dividing by the live scale puts every line on its exact benchmark pixel on every object.
- Added: the oracle asserts each of the five weights to +/-0.06 px per object, that the silhouette is strictly heavier than EVERY other line (internal edges included), that each view has exactly one silhouette, that internal geometry actually reaches the DOM, and that **no drawn-on stroke is non-scaling** - the last being the assertion that would have caught the regression above. 137 assertions green.

Measured on the shipped sheet, the visible outline rendered at **2.23 px** on one object and **2.13 px** on a wider one against `graphics_module_1_topic_1_1_dimensioning`'s **2.5**; hidden came out 1.25-1.31 against 1.5, centre 1.00-1.05 against 1.3, and Type B 0.70-0.84 against 1.0. ## 2026-08-06 (h)
- Changed: in `graphics_module_2_topic_0_introduction_to_orthographic_projection`, the two annotation switches **swapped places**. The floating chip is now **Dimensions** - the sizes are read on the model, so the control for them belongs where the model is - and **Front arrow** moved into the step card beside the four direction buttons, because it is another viewing aid. Both keep the benchmark's `.vp-chip` / `.switch-row` components; only which state each drives has changed.
- Changed: the two are now **independent flags**. Dimensions no longer takes the arrow with it, and the arrow's switch no longer touches the sizes. A learner reading sizes off a clean model and a learner checking which way the front is are not the same learner, and neither should have to turn on the other's marks.
- Changed: the **arrow is no longer gated to the front view**. It is parented inside the object's own group, so it rides with the model and marks the front face from any angle, free orbit included. Gating it to the front view meant the one direction where it is degenerate - pointing straight at the camera and foreshortened to a dot - was the only direction it ever appeared in. ADR-132 amended rather than silently changed (RULES.md 8.4).
- Changed: dimension linework on the solid now draws with `depthTest: false` and `renderOrder = 2`, and stands 10 mm off the face rather than 6. A dimension is annotation, not a feature competing for depth with the part: half an arrowhead swallowed by a boss is a dimension the learner cannot check, and the values are CSS2D and were never occluded anyway, so depth-testing only the linework made the two halves of one mark disagree.
- Note: the arrowheads stay the **3:1 open chevron**. RULES.md 6.19's carve-out for 15 degree heads (ADR-079) is scoped to `graphics_module_1_topic_1_1_dimensioning`, which TEACHES termination geometry; dimensions here are incidental, so 3:1 is the style that rule requires - copying the benchmark's heads would have broken it, not matched it.
- Verified: 117 assertions green, including the arrow present in all four directions and free orbit, each switch leaving the other's marks alone, and per-view dimension counts audited against the registry.
