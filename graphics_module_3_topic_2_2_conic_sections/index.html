    Eccentricity e —

    Why the curve changes

    Engineers describe every one of these curves with a single number — the eccentricity. It says how far the cutting plane has leaned away from the base. Tilt the plane and that number changes, and when it passes certain values the curve becomes a different kind of curve altogether. That is where the four names come from.

    Circle e = 0
    The plane cuts parallel to the base. Perfectly round.
    Ellipse 0 < e < 1
    The plane is tilted, but not past the cone's own sloping side. The curve closes.
    Parabola e = 1
    The plane is parallel to one side of the cone. The curve never closes.
    Hyperbola e > 1
    The plane is steeper still and reaches the second half of the cone. Two branches.
    Now the names. That ratio is the , written e. The fixed point is the and the fixed line the .

    You have understood why the curve forms. Now learn how it is constructed on paper — scale, compass, set square. No cone from here.
