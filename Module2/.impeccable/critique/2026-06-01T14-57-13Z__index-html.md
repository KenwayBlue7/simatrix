## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated? No.** It passes both the general slop test and the product-register test. The design is committed to a specific north star ("The Patient Tutor's Paper"): a warm drafting-paper palette, flat ink-on-surface depth, one rationed blue accent, color-blind-safe teal/amber domain encodings each paired with a second cue. None of the absolute bans are present — no gradient text, no glassmorphism, no side-stripe borders, no hero-metric template, no identical card grid, no modal-first reflex. A user fluent in GeoGebra / Desmos / Falstad would sit down and trust it.

## Overall Impression

This is a mature, design-system-faithful build that takes its own PRODUCT.md and DESIGN.md seriously — progressive disclosure is real, the rotation hierarchy is enforced in the UI, color is read from tokens, reduced-motion is honored, and the screen-reader story is unusually thoughtful for a 3D sim. The biggest opportunity is protecting the anxious primary persona from the two "did I break it?" moments the current build still allows: a one-click Reset that wipes six steps of work, and a drawing that silently unfolds when they nudge a slider.

## What's Working

1. **Progressive disclosure is genuine, not cosmetic.** The scene boots empty (grids only), each step reveals exactly its controls, and `rebuild()` honors the wizard flags so editing an early step reflows later output non-destructively. This is the Guided-Stepper promise actually delivered.
2. **The two-cue accessibility discipline holds throughout.** HP = teal + solid + "HP" label; VP = amber + dashed + "VP" label; success = green + check glyph; disabled = faint + padlock icon. Color never carries meaning alone, and it is consistent across every surface.
3. **The voice matches the brand.** Step leads explain the *why* before the *how* ("The views you draw next depend on how it sits"), terms are defined in plain language without being dumbed down, and announcements are calm and specific.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Reset is a one-click, unconfirmed total wipe
- **Why it matters:** The ghost "Reset" button sits in the card nav on every step. A struggling first-year who has completed five steps can destroy all of it — solid, labels, projections, fold — with a single stray click, no confirmation and no undo. This is the exact anxiety spike ("I broke it and can't get it back") the product is built to prevent.
- **Fix:** Wrap the in-sim Reset button in a lightweight inline confirm (a two-state "Reset everything? · Yes / Cancel" on the same ghost control) or an undo toast. Keep `window.simAPI.reset()` as the single reset path — the confirmation guards the button, not the platform call.
- **Suggested command:** `harden`

### [P1] Editing geometry while flattened unfolds the drawing with no visible explanation
- **Why it matters:** `reflowFrom()` calls `sim.unflatten()` and announces "Unfolded to 3D so you can see your change" — but only through the `sr-only` live region. A sighted learner who nudges a slider on Step 1/2 after flattening sees their finished 2D drawing suddenly spring back to 3D for no apparent reason. Status that exists for screen readers but not for sighted users is a visibility gap.
- **Fix:** Surface the same message as a brief visible inline note or viewport toast (reusing the `--color-accent-soft` hint styling), in step with the unfold, then auto-dismiss.
- **Suggested command:** `clarify`

### [P2] Interactive targets fall below the design system's own 44px floor
- **Why it matters:** DESIGN.md and PRODUCT.md both mandate a 44px minimum target "so an anxious mouse, a touch screen, or the back row of a classroom projector can all reach it." Yet the viewport quick-view chips are `height: 30px`, the connector-toggle row is `30px` with a `14px` checkbox, the wizard toggle is `40px`, and the orbit-hint dismiss is `28px`. These clear WCAG 2.2 AA's 24px minimum but violate the project's self-imposed accessibility bar, on exactly the controls a touch or back-row user reaches for.
- **Fix:** Raise the viewport chips and dismiss buttons to 44px (or pad a 44px hit area around the smaller visual element, as the sliders already do).
- **Suggested command:** `adapt`

### [P2] `aria-live="polite"` on the entire `#step-card` over-announces for screen readers
- **Why it matters:** The whole step card is a live region (`index.html:1072`), so on every step transition the screen reader may re-read the entire swapped-in panel — and this duplicates the dedicated `#sim-status` region, which already announces "Step X of N." Large containers as live regions are a known a11y anti-pattern: verbose, and they fire on value-text updates too.
- **Fix:** Remove `aria-live` from `#step-card` and let the dedicated `#sim-status` region own all announcements (it already does, via `sim.announce`).
- **Suggested command:** `harden`

### [P2] `font-weight: 600` violates the Two-Weight Rule
- **Why it matters:** Atkinson Hyperlegible ships Regular (400) and Bold (700) only; DESIGN.md explicitly forbids building hierarchy "from a 500/600 weight that does not exist." `.dock__group-title`, `.quick-view`, and `.connector-toggle` all set `font-weight: 600`, so the browser either synthesizes a faux-bold or falls back inconsistently — the labels render at an unintended weight.
- **Fix:** Move these to `700` (and dial back size/letter-spacing if 700 reads too heavy) or to `400`.
- **Suggested command:** `typeset`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (Confused First-Timer):** Mostly well served — the first action is obvious (empty-state points at the panel), terms are defined, every step explains itself. Red flags: an accidental click on the always-present Reset wipes everything with no confirm; and the silent unfold-on-edit reads as "something went wrong" with no visible message.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent):** Genuinely strong — focus rings everywhere, `aria-valuetext` on sliders, reduced-motion honored, a dedicated live region. Red flags: the `aria-live` container on `#step-card` makes step changes verbose and double-announced; the step rail is not keyboard-operable, so Sam can't jump back to a completed step (Back-only, one at a time); revisiting Step 2 from Step 6 is four key-presses through buttons.

**Priya (project persona — anxious struggling first-year):** The tone and pacing are built for her, and they land. The two places she still gets hurt are the unconfirmed Reset and the unexplained unfold — both are precisely the moments that confirm her fear that she is "bad at this."

## Minor Observations

- The step rail is display-only; making completed/current markers focusable buttons that jump to that step (forward still gated by completion) would add safe flexibility without breaking the guided model.
- By Step 6 up to five floating controls sit over the viewport (wizard toggle, three-button quick-view cluster, connector toggle, first-angle symbol, plus the transient orbit hint). Each is small and purposeful, but the top-left cluster could be grouped.
- Invalid numeric entry reverts silently to the last valid value. A tiny non-alarming nudge ("kept 2.0") would close the error-recovery gap without reaching for red.

## Questions to Consider

- What would the most reassuring possible version of "Reset" look like for a student who just spent ten minutes building a drawing?
- Should the rail be a map the learner can move around, or strictly a one-way progress spine? The current build is the latter; is that a deliberate constraint or an accident of scope?
- The viewport corner controls are correct individually. Are they correct *collectively* at Step 6, or is that the one screen where the "quiet chrome" rule quietly slips?
