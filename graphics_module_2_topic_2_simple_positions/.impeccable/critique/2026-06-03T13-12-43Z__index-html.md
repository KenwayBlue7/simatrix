## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated?** No. The feature reads as a deliberate, on-system addition: tokenized, flat, monochrome chrome with one accent, the focus-trapped overlay, the gentle self-check. It passes the product slop test (a category-fluent user would trust it).

**LLM assessment:** The one tell is the entry button. A lone ghost link floating in an otherwise empty band at the top of the panel is the classic "bolt the new feature on top" move. It has no container, no alignment partner, and acres of empty space to its right.

**Deterministic scan:** Unavailable. `detect.mjs` ran but reported "bundled detector not found" (exit 1) in this install, so no automated findings. Manual + rendered-screenshot review used instead.

**Visual evidence:** Headless-Chrome screenshots of the booted (empty-start) and problem-loaded states. No live in-page overlay was injected (the detector bundle is absent), so there is no `[Human]` overlay tab; the evidence is the two PNG renders.

## Overall Impression

The library itself is strong: it works, it teaches, it stays quiet. The single biggest opportunity is the one the user already feels: the **Practice problems entry has no home.** It currently lives above the wizard column as a free-floating ghost, which (a) steals the panel's opening moment from the guided flow, (b) pushes the rail + step card down so their top edge no longer aligns with the viewport's Top/Front/Side controls, and (c) sits orphaned above the bordered problem header once a problem is loaded. Anchor it and the feature reads as native.

## What's Working

- **The active-problem header.** Bordered accent-wash band with eyebrow, statement, "Hide text", self-check status, and "Exit problem" reads as intentional and anchored. It is the model the entry button should aspire to.
- **The self-check copy.** "Add the solid in Step 1, then set it to match the problem" then a green check on match: calm, specific, never red. On-persona for the anxious first-year.
- **Restraint.** It resisted emoji, a viewport banner, and difficulty badges. The overlay is flat and grouped, not a shadow-heavy card showroom.

## Priority Issues

- **[P1] The entry button is unanchored and steals the panel's opening.** It floats in a ~56px band above the rail/card, left-aligned with a wide empty gutter, and is the literal first thing in the tutor column on boot, ahead of "Step 1". 
  - **Why it matters:** It breaks "orient: start at one meaningful step" (PRODUCT.md), reads as bolted-on (AI-slop tell), and misaligns the whole guided column against the viewport's top controls. Consistency (H4) and minimalism (H8) both take the hit.
  - **Fix:** Give it a real anchor. Strongest option: a quiet ghost link right-aligned on the "STEP X OF 6" eyebrow row inside the card, so the card top realigns with the viewport controls and the entry shares a baseline instead of floating. Alternatives: a panel footer, or fold the idle entry and the loaded-problem header into one band.
  - **Suggested command:** `layout`

- **[P2] Stacked redundancy in the loaded state.** With a problem active, the column shows "Practice problems" (entry) directly above the "Textbook problem" header, two problem-related bands stacked, the top one orphaned.
  - **Why it matters:** Two affordances about the same concept compete; the loaded problem header already owns the context and its own exit.
  - **Fix:** Use one slot: when a problem is loaded, the entry yields to the header (and offers "Change problem" from within the header), rather than persisting above it.
  - **Suggested command:** `layout`

- **[P3] Loaded state leans blue.** The accent-soft problem header is a tall tinted block sitting near the accent primary button and the accent rail marker; together they push the "blue ≤10%" budget in that column.
  - **Why it matters:** DESIGN.md Quiet-Chrome Rule; the eye should land on the viewport.
  - **Fix:** Consider a neutral (paper + hairline) treatment for the problem header, reserving accent-soft for the self-check success only, or shorten/clamp the statement by default.
  - **Suggested command:** `quieter`

## Persona Red Flags

**Asha (anxious first-year, the primary persona):** Opens the sim; the first line in the guidance panel is "Practice problems", not "Step 1 — Add a solid". For a learner who assumes they're "bad at this", being pointed at exam practice before the lesson is a small jolt. The guided flow should clearly own the opening.

**Instructor on a projector:** Demonstrating from the back of the room, the floating ghost link is low-contrast (ink-secondary) and easy to miss as an affordance; meanwhile it occupies prime top-of-panel space. Either commit it to a clearer affordance or move it out of the opening.

**Confident student (power user):** Wants to jump straight to practice. The entry is discoverable enough, but once in a problem there is no "switch problem" without "Exit problem" first, a small extra step.

## Minor Observations

- The entry stays labeled "Practice problems" even while a problem is loaded; it could reflect state ("Change problem") if kept visible.
- The ~56px top band is dead space on boot; reclaiming it lets the card/rail align with the viewport chrome.
- The book glyph is fine, but at ink-secondary on paper its affordance (is it a button?) is faint for a first-timer.

## Questions to Consider

- What if the guided flow unambiguously owned the top of the panel, and practice was a quiet, anchored secondary action rather than a floating peer?
- Should the idle entry and the active-problem header be the same slot, so the column never stacks two problem bands?
- Where would a confident student instinctively look for "practice problems": the panel header, a footer, or back on the viewport?
